Arena Value Wizard — Help & Guide

Step 1 of 9

Company Profile

This step captures the foundational data that drives every downstream calculation in the Arena Value Assessment.

Inputs

FieldTypeWhy It Matters
Customer Name Text Labels the output report. Does not affect calculations.
Annual Revenue ($) Currency The single most important input. Revenue is the base for all revenue-side benefits: Time-to-Market, Direct Material savings, CM Material savings, and more.
Total Employees Number Multiplied by each team's FTE percentage (Step 4) to produce FTE counts. Those FTE counts then multiply by salary and efficiency improvement to produce labor dollar savings.
Tip: Use the most recent fiscal year's figures. Revenue and headcount are the two anchors that size every benefit — accuracy here means accuracy everywhere.
Step 2 of 9

Business Assumptions

These assumptions shape the revenue-side benefit calculations, particularly the Time-to-Market benefit.

Inputs

FieldRangeHow It Feeds Calculations
Revenue Growth Rate 0–50% Feeds the Time-to-Market benefit. Faster product launch means capturing more of this growth revenue before competitors.
% Revenue from New Products 0–100% Weights how much of total revenue comes from new or recently launched products — the portion most sensitive to launch timing.
Profit Margin on New Products 0–50% The margin applied to incremental early-launch revenue. Higher margins amplify the value of getting to market sooner.
NPD/NPI Cycle Time (weeks) 4–260 wks Your baseline development cycle. The Time-to-Market improvement percentage reduces this to calculate weeks saved, which translates to revenue captured earlier.
Example: If your NPD cycle is 52 weeks and Arena helps reduce it by 15%, that's ~8 weeks faster to market — potentially capturing weeks of premium pricing that would otherwise go to competitors.

Product Data Consumers (PDC)

PDCs have an additional "% Workweek Consuming Data" slider. This adjusts the effective labor base — if PDCs only spend 10% of their time on product data, only that fraction is eligible for improvement.

Include / Exclude Toggle

Each benefit row has a Yes/No toggle. Excluded rows write "No" to column F in the Benefits Calc tab and are not counted in the total. Use this to remove benefits that don't apply to the customer's situation.

Maturity Levels

Each benefit has two sliders: Today (without Arena) shows what your current system already delivers, and With Arena (Target) is where Arena will take you. The Net benefit — the difference between the two — is what gets written to the spreadsheet and drives the ROI calculation.

Each slider has 4 maturity checkpoints shown as dots on the track. As you move the slider, the maturity description updates to reflect the customer's current PLM adoption level.

Step 8 of 9

Legacy System Costs

Capture the total cost of your current PLM/QMS system so the spreadsheet can calculate Arena's total cost of ownership (TCO) advantage.

Line Items

RowWhat to enter
PLM Software License Per-seat annual license fee × number of seats (default: excluded — check Include if applicable)
Support & Maintenance Annual support contract — typically 18–22% of license cost
Integrations Annual cost per integration connector (CAD, ERP, Reporting tools)
IT Infrastructure Servers, backup, security, managed services
IT Labor Internal IT staff time: BDA, admin, hardware support
Upgrades & Training Annual upgrade costs: hardware refreshes, software updates, training
Other Tools Third-party tools (e.g. SiliconExpert, component intelligence platforms)

How Each Row Works

Each row has three inputs: Unit Cost (annual $ per unit), Quantity (number of units or a percentage multiplier), and an Include toggle. Excluded rows write "No" to the sheet and are not counted in the TCO total.

On-Prem / Perpetual toggle: The toggle at the top of Step 8 writes "Yes" or "No" to cell E3 of the Legacy TCO tab. Set it to "Yes" if the customer owns a perpetual on-premise license. This flag is used by TCO formulas in the spreadsheet.

Support & Maintenance — Multiplier Note

The Support row uses a percentage multiplier for Quantity (e.g. enter 20 for 20%). The sheet stores this as a decimal (0.20) and applies it to the license cost. This means you can leave Quantity at 20 even as license costs change — the formula recalculates automatically.

Step 9 of 9

Review & Submit

This final step shows a summary of all values before writing them to the spreadsheet. Review everything carefully before submitting.

What Happens on Submit

  • Data Input tab, Column E — All company profile, assumptions, cost structure, and team resource values are written (rows E3–E48).
  • Benefits Calc tab, Column D — Improvement percentages for each benefit (stored as decimals, e.g. 15% becomes 0.15).
  • Benefits Calc tab, Column F — "Yes" or "No" for each benefit's include/exclude toggle.
  • SpreadsheetApp.flush() — Forces Google Sheets to recalculate all formula cells immediately.
  • Column G auto-updates — All benefit dollar amounts in column G recalculate instantly based on your new inputs.
Safe to re-run: You can submit multiple times. Each submit overwrites the previous values. Use "Clear All Inputs" from the Arena menu to reset everything back to blank.

After Submitting

Close the wizard and review the spreadsheet. The Benefits Summary and ROI Output sections will reflect your inputs immediately. You can reopen the wizard at any time from the Arena menu to adjust values.

How Benefits are Calculated (Column G)

Column G cells contain formulas that combine:

  • Base values from the Data Input tab (Revenue, FTEs, Salaries, Costs)
  • Improvement percentages from Benefits Calc column D
  • Include/Exclude flags from Benefits Calc column F

When a benefit is set to "No" (excluded), the column G formula returns $0 for that row regardless of the improvement percentage.

Key insight: The wizard only writes raw inputs and improvement percentages. All dollar benefit calculations happen via spreadsheet formulas — ensuring full transparency and auditability of the ROI model.
